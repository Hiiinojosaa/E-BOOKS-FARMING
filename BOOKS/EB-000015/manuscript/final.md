# Introduction

This book contains 9 sudoku puzzles, sorted from easy to hard, each with exactly one solution
and verified before going to print. No guessing is required: every puzzle in this book can be
solved through logic alone.

Puzzles are grouped into three sections by difficulty — Easy, Medium, and Hard — so you can
warm up on the easier grids before moving on to the more challenging ones. Every solution is in
the Solutions chapter at the back of the book, listed in the same order as the puzzles, so you
can check your work or get unstuck without accidentally seeing the answer to the next puzzle on
the page.

The grids in this book are printed large and clear so they are comfortable to read and fill in.
Each grid is a standard 9x9 sudoku. You only need a pencil with an eraser — no app, no timer,
and no score. Work through the puzzles at whatever pace suits you best, and use the Solutions
section as often as you like. The point is to enjoy the puzzle, not to finish it quickly.

# How to Solve

Each grid is nine rows and nine columns, divided into nine 3x3 boxes. Fill every empty cell with
a digit from 1 to 9 so that each row, each column, and each 3x3 box contains every digit exactly
once. A finished puzzle never repeats a digit in the same row, column, or box.

Start with the rows, columns, or boxes that already have the most numbers filled in: they leave
the fewest open cells, so the correct digit is often forced. For any empty cell, look at its row,
its column, and its box together; if only one digit from 1 to 9 is missing from all three, that
digit belongs there.

When no cell is immediately forced, pick a digit and scan the whole grid for the one box, row, or
column where it can only go in a single remaining cell, even if that cell isn't obvious at first
glance. Working digit by digit like this, rather than cell by cell, often breaks a puzzle open
faster than staring at one stubborn square.

If you get stuck, it's rarely because a puzzle has no logical path forward: it usually means a
digit was placed too early based on a guess. Double-check your filled-in numbers against their
row, column, and box before assuming the puzzle is unsolvable, and the stuck point almost always
resolves itself.


# Easy Puzzles

These three grids are the best place to start if you are new to sudoku or if you want to warm up before moving on to harder puzzles. Easy grids have more digits already filled in, which means fewer cells to figure out and more clear-cut places to begin. Work through them at your own pace — there is no time limit, and the logic needed is always straightforward.

Each puzzle has exactly one correct solution, which you will find in the Solutions chapter at the back of the book. If you get stuck at any point, try scanning a single digit across the whole grid to find the one cell where it can only go in one place. That single placement often unlocks a chain of others and opens the puzzle back up. Use a pencil so you can erase if you need to, and do not worry about starting over — coming back to a puzzle fresh sometimes makes the solution much more obvious than continuing to stare at a stuck grid.

# Medium Puzzles

The three puzzles in this section have fewer starting digits than the easy ones, which means more cells to work out on your own. The solving technique is the same — check rows, columns, and boxes — but you will need to apply it more patiently, scanning each digit in turn rather than finding fills quickly. Do not be discouraged if it takes longer than the easy grids. That is the point of medium difficulty: it stretches the same skill set without requiring any new technique.

Take a break between puzzles if you need one. The best time to step away from a grid is when you feel stuck and frustrated rather than stuck and curious — coming back after a short rest often lets you spot a digit that was invisible before. The solutions are at the back of the book in the same order — Puzzles 4, 5, and 6 — so you can check your work or get unstuck without seeing the answers to the harder puzzles that come next.

# Hard Puzzles

These three grids start with the fewest digits filled in, which gives you the most to figure out. The rules and technique are identical to the easy and medium puzzles: no guessing is ever required, and each puzzle has exactly one true solution that logic alone can reach. What changes is the patience needed to find the first forced cell, since it may not be immediately obvious.

If you find yourself going in circles, the most useful reset is to pick one digit from 1 to 9 and scan every row, column, and box for that single digit only. This single-digit scan often reveals a placement that was hidden when you were looking at the whole grid at once. After you place that digit, the ripple of newly forced cells usually makes the next few placements clearer. Puzzles 7, 8, and 9 are in the Solutions section at the back — check them whenever you need to, without any guilt about it.

# Solutions

Every solution below was verified by a solver before this book went to print, confirming that each puzzle has exactly one correct answer. The solutions are in the same order as the puzzles — Easy first, then Medium, then Hard — so you can check Puzzle 1 without accidentally seeing Puzzle 9. If your filled-in grid does not match the solution, look for the first cell that differs and trace back through that row, column, and box to find where a digit was placed too early. In most cases, a single misplaced digit early in the puzzle causes a chain of incorrect fills that is easy to unravel once you find the source. Use the solutions freely: getting unstuck and finishing a puzzle is always more satisfying than leaving one incomplete.
