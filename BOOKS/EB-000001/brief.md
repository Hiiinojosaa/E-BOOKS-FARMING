# Brief — EB-000001

## Working title
**The One-Page System** — A Productivity Method for Solo Freelancers

## Audience
Solo freelancers and independent contractors (designers, writers, developers, consultants)
who work alone, without a manager, a team, or built-in office structure. They already know the
usual advice (to-do lists, time blocking, habit tracking) but still feel scattered, because
nothing external forces a rhythm onto their day. They're frustrated by productivity books that
are either too generic ("build better habits") or too heavy (multi-week courses, big toolkits
of apps). They want one simple way to start the day knowing what matters, and to end it without
work bleeding into personal time.

## Promise
Readers will walk away with a single one-page system they can rebuild every morning in under
five minutes — no app, no course, no dashboard — that tells them what to do first, what to
ignore, and when to stop.

Three measurable results:
1. A reusable one-page daily template the reader can redraw from memory.
2. A method to pick the day's top 1–3 priorities in under five minutes, even with multiple clients.
3. A clear, repeatable way to close the workday so it doesn't bleed into personal time.

## Differentiation
From research: existing freelancer-productivity titles are either broad AI/tool playbooks or
multi-week time-management courses — not a short, single-method guide. This book is explicitly
the opposite: one method, one page, no tool stack, no subscription, readable in one sitting and
usable the same day. It targets solo freelancers specifically, not "productivity" in general.

## Outline
1. **Introduction — Why Structure Doesn't Come Free When You Work Alone** — objective: name the
   real problem (no external structure, not lack of willpower); key points: office structure
   freelancers lose, why generic habit advice doesn't fix it; no exercise (sets up the rest).
2. **The One-Page System, at a Glance** — objective: show the whole method on one page before
   explaining the parts; key points: the four zones of the page (Top priorities, Client map,
   Time blocks, Day-close line); exercise: sketch a blank version of the page.
3. **Choosing What Actually Goes First** — objective: a fast method to pick 1–3 priorities across
   multiple clients without a project-management suite; key points: separating "urgent for a
   client" from "important for the business," a 5-minute morning filter; exercise: fill the
   Top Priorities zone for tomorrow.
4. **Protecting Deep Work at Home** — objective: give a concrete way to block distraction-prone
   hours; key points: naming your actual distraction windows, a simple visible signal (to
   yourself, not a team) for "do not disturb"; exercise: mark two protected blocks on the page.
5. **Mapping Clients Without a Dashboard** — objective: track multiple clients/projects on paper
   without extra software; key points: the Client Map zone, a weekly five-minute reset; exercise:
   fill the Client Map zone with current clients/projects.
6. **Closing the Day on Purpose** — objective: stop work from bleeding into personal time; key
   points: the Day-close line, a 2-minute end-of-day ritual, deciding tomorrow's first task
   tonight; exercise: write today's Day-close line.
7. **Conclusion — Making the Page a Habit** — objective: turn the one-page system into a standing
   routine; key points: redrawing the page daily vs. printing a template, what to do on chaotic
   days, a short recap of the four zones.

## Tone
Second person ("you"), direct and practical, short sentences, no corporate jargon. Encouraging
but not motivational-poster fluff. Avoid absolute claims ("this will fix your life") — keep
promises concrete and modest, matching what a one-page method can realistically deliver.

## Length
6000 words total (Introduction and Conclusion ~500–600 words each; Chapters 2–6 ~900–1000
words each).
